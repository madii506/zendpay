zend:~$ man zend # 08

straight answers.

The on-chain link between your wallet and theirs. You pay a one-time NEAR Intents address and solvers pay your recipient from their own funds. It doesn't hide amounts, and the route sees both ends. It is not a zero-knowledge confidential transfer.

Not Zend. A sealed zend waits at its one-time address for about 20 seconds while solvers fill it. If it can't be filled, it refunds to your wallet. A link holds its SOL under its own key, and that key exists only in the link.

Your browser makes a fresh key and puts it after the # in the link. That part of a URL never reaches any server. You fund the key, share the link, and whoever opens it can claim to any wallet. Until then you can take it back from your zends on this device.

Sealed: the route's spread and fee, usually 0.1–0.3%, shown before you sign. USDC to a new address also pays about 0.002 SOL to open its token account. Open sends and links: only the network fee. Zend adds nothing.

Links need at least 0.002 SOL so the account can exist. Very small sealed zends can be refused by the solvers, and the quote tells you before you sign.
